## Celestial Coordinates, Angles & House Systems

1. **Alchabitius Houses** — An intermediate house system used heavily in medieval Arabic and Persian astrological texts.
2. **Angle** — One of the four most powerful and sensitive points in an astrological chart: the Ascendant, Descendant, Midheaven, and Imum Coeli.
3. **Angular House** — The 1st, 4th, 7th, and 10th houses of a birth chart, associated in astrology with immediate action, high energy, and the foundational pillars of life.
4. **Angular Houses** — Houses 1, 4, 7, and 10; sectors of high initiative, visibility, and immediate external action.
5. **Antapex** — A calculated astronomical or astrological point opposite an apex, occasionally used in specialized predictive work.
6. **Ascendant (ASC) (Rising Sign)** — The sign and degree of the ecliptic rising on the eastern horizon at the moment of calculation; defines the 1st house cusp.
7. **Ascensional Times** — The length of time it takes for a sign of the zodiac to rise over the horizon at a specific geographical latitude, used in ancient house systems.
8. **Astronomy** — The scientific study of the universe and celestial objects, which provides the precise mathematical framework and coordinate systems used by astrologers to cast charts.
9. **Autumnal Equinox** — The astronomical point when the Sun crosses the celestial equator going south, marking the beginning of Libra and the astrological autumn.
10. **Axis** — A polarity connecting two opposite signs or houses in an astrology chart, such as the Aries-Libra axis or the 1st-7th house axis.
11. **Ayana** — A Sanskrit term used in Vedic astrology to describe the Sun's northward or southward movement.
12. **Bhava** — The term for an astrological house in Vedic astrology.
13. **Cadent House** — The 3rd, 6th, 9th, and 12th houses of a birth chart, traditionally associated in astrology with transition, processing, adaptation, and mental integration.
14. **Cadent Houses** — Houses 3, 6, 9, and 12; sectors of transition, adaptation, learning, and mental processing.
15. **Campanus House System** — House division based on trisecting the prime vertical into equal 30° arcs.
16. **Celestial Equator** — The projection of Earth's terrestrial equator into space, serving as the reference plane for equatorial coordinates.
17. **Celestial Poles** — The points north and south where Earth's axis of rotation intersects the celestial sphere.
18. **Celestial Sphere** — An imaginary giant sphere surrounding Earth upon which planets and stars appear to move, forming the spatial geometry of astrology.
19. **Circumpolar** — Stars or celestial points that never rise or set relative to a specific observer's latitude on Earth.
20. **Cusp** — The dividing line between two zodiac signs or two astrological houses.
21. **Cuspal Degree** — The precise starting degree of a house or zodiac sign.
22. **Declination (Dec)** — The angular distance of a celestial body north (+) or south (-) of the celestial equator.
23. **Descendant (DSC)** — The sign and degree setting on the western horizon (180° opposite the Ascendant); defines the 7th house cusp.
24. **Diurnal Arc** — The path and time duration a celestial body spends above the horizon between rising and setting.
25. **Duplicated Sign** — A sign containing two house cusps due to distortion in high-latitude quadrant house calculation.
26. **Ecliptic** — The apparent path of the Sun across the celestial sphere over the course of a year; the central plane of the zodiac.
27. **Ecliptic Latitude (β)** — The angular distance of a body north or south of the ecliptic plane.
28. **Ecliptic Longitude (λ)** — The angular distance along the ecliptic measured eastward from 0° Aries.
29. **Eighth House** — The house of shared resources, transformation, sexuality, debts, and psychological depth. It deals with profound changes, inheritances, and deep psychological merging.
30. **Elevation** — A planet's proximity to the Midheaven or its vertical height relative to other planets in the chart, often granting it greater visibility or influence.
31. **Eleventh House** — The house of friendships, group affiliations, community, and future hopes. It highlights collective endeavors, networking, and social ideals.
32. **Ephemeris** — A reference table or book listing the daily calculated positions of the planets, Sun, Moon, and other astrological points over a given time period.
33. **Equal House System** — System where houses are divided into equal 30° segments starting from the exact degree of the Ascendant.
34. **Equator** — The great circle equidistant from the poles of a celestial body.
35. **Equatorial Coordinates** — Celestial measurement using right ascension and declination, independent of the horizon.
36. **Equinoctial Points** — The two points where the ecliptic intersects the celestial equator (0° Aries and 0° Libra).
37. **Equinox** — Either of the two times each year when day and night are of approximately equal length, marking the transition into Aries and Libra.
38. **Fifth House** — The house of creativity, romance, pleasure, children, and hobbies. It signifies self-expression, playfulness, and what brings joy to an individual.
39. **First House** — The house of self, physical appearance, and initial impressions, beginning with the ascendant. It represents how a person approaches the world and projects their identity to others.
40. **First Point of Aries** — The exact 0-degree point of Aries, marking the vernal equinox and the start of the tropical zodiac.
41. **First Point of Cancer** — The 0-degree point of Cancer, marking the summer solstice.
42. **First Point of Capricorn** — The 0-degree point of Capricorn, marking the winter solstice.
43. **First Point of Libra** — The 0-degree point of Libra, marking the autumn equinox.
44. **Fourth House** — The house of home, family roots, ancestral background, and private life. It reflects the emotional foundation and the private inner sanctuary of a person.
45. **Heliacal Rising** — The astronomical event when a planet first becomes visible in the eastern dawn sky just before sunrise after a period of invisibility. In traditional astrology, it marks a powerful renewal of that planet's visibility and strength.
46. **Heliacal Setting** — The moment a planet disappears into the brilliant rays of the evening or morning sun, becoming invisible for a period. It symbolizes a time of retreat, digestion, or incubation for that planet's themes.
47. **Hemisphere** — One half of an astrological chart (Northern, Southern, Eastern, or Western), used to determine whether a person's focus is more internal, external, social, or self-contained.
48. **Hora** — A traditional 1-hour planetary period division of the day, or a divisional half-sign chart in Vedic astrology.
49. **Horizon** — The great circle perpendicular to the line connecting the observer's Zenith and Nadir.
50. **Hour Circle** — A great circle on the celestial sphere passing through the celestial poles, used in house system calculations.
51. **House Cusp** — The boundary degree marking the beginning of one of the 12 astrological houses.
52. **House System** — The mathematical method used to divide the 360-degree chart wheel into twelve distinct life sectors (houses), such as Placidus, Whole Sign, or Koch.
53. **Houses** — The twelve architectural sectors of a birth chart, each governing specific life domains such as career, relationships, home, and health.
54. **Imum Coeli (IC)** — The nadir or lowest point of the ecliptic wheel (180° opposite the Midheaven); defines the 4th house cusp.
55. **Intercepted Planet** — A planet located within an intercepted sign.
56. **Intercepted Sign** — A zodiac sign that lies entirely within a single house without containing a house cusp.
57. **Interception** — A zodiac sign that falls entirely within a single astrological house without touching either cusp, often associated with delayed expression or hidden themes in modern house systems.
58. **Interpolation** — The mathematical method used by astrologers to estimate planet positions between the tabulated dates in an ephemeris.
59. **Koch House System** — Quadrant system based on trisecting the diurnal arc of the Midheaven.
60. **Koch-Alcabitus** — A conflation of terms; Alcabitus is an independent historical medieval house system that divides the diurnal arc of the ascendant into proportional parts, predating Placidus.
61. **Latitude** — The angular distance north or south of Earth's equator, used alongside longitude to calculate accurate house cusps for a birth chart.
62. **Local Sidereal Time (LST)** — Time measured relative to the vernal equinox; used directly in calculating house cusps.
63. **Longitude** — The angular distance measured eastward along the celestial equator, used to determine the exact degree of planets and points within the zodiac.
64. **Meridian** — The great circle passing through the celestial poles and the observer's Zenith and Nadir.
65. **Midheaven (MC)** — _Medium Coeli_; the point where the ecliptic intersects the local meridian; defines the 10th house cusp in most quadrant systems.
66. **Minute of Arc** — A unit of angular measurement equal to one-sixtieth of a degree, used for pinpointing exact planetary positions.
67. **Morinus House System** — System based on equal 30° divisions of the celestial equator projected onto the ecliptic.
68. **Nadir** — The point directly below an observer on Earth, opposite the Zenith.
69. **Ninth House** — The house of higher education, long-distance travel, philosophy, publishing, and spiritual exploration. It represents the quest for meaning and broader horizons.
70. **Nocturnal Arc** — The path and time duration a celestial body spends below the horizon between setting and rising.
71. **Nutational Movement** — A slight wobble in Earth's axis of rotation that astronomers factor in for extreme precision in celestial mapping.
72. **Oblique Ascension (OA)** — The degree of the celestial equator that rises alongside a point on the ecliptic at a specific geographical latitude.
73. **Oblique Descension (OD)** — The degree of the celestial equator that sets alongside a point on the ecliptic at a specific geographical latitude.
74. **Obliquity (ε)** — The angle of Earth's axial tilt (approximately 23.44°), determining the intersection between the ecliptic and celestial equator.
75. **Placidus House System** — Quadrant house division system based on trisection of semi-arcs of time.
76. **Porphyry House System** — Simple quadrant system dividing each quadrant between ASC and MC into three equal arcs.
77. **Prime Vertical** — The great circle passing through the Zenith, Nadir, East point, and West point of the horizon.
78. **Primum Mobile** — An ancient cosmological concept of an outermost moving sphere that drives all lower celestial movements.
79. **Quadrant** — One of the four quarters of an astrological chart defined by the four angles (Ascendant, IC, Descendant, Midheaven).
80. **Quadrant Houses** — House systems like Placidus or Koch that divide space based on the quadrants of the sky and horizon.
81. **RAMC** — Right Ascension of the Midheaven; the equatorial coordinate derived from local sidereal time.
82. **Regiomontanus House System** — Quadrant house system dividing the celestial equator into 30° arcs projected onto the ecliptic through horizon intersections.
83. **Right Ascension (RA)** — The angular distance measured eastward along the celestial equator from the vernal equinox, expressed in degrees or hours.
84. **Second House** — The house of personal resources, money, self-worth, and material possessions. It highlights what a person values and how they manage their tangible assets.
85. **Semi-Arc** — Half of a body's diurnal or nocturnal arc; fundamental to Placidus house division and primary directions.
86. **Seventh House** — The house of one-on-one partnerships, marriage, open enemies, and business contracts. It explores how a person relates to significant others and mirrors aspects of themselves in others.
87. **Sixth House** — The house of daily routines, work, health, and service to others. It addresses habits, wellness practices, and the mechanics of daily labor.
88. **Solar Houses** — A simplified chart method placing the Sun in the first house and reading subsequent signs as houses.
89. **Solstice** — The two times each year when the Sun reaches its maximum northern or southern declination, marking the start of Cancer and Capricorn.
90. **Speculum** — A technical data table listing the precise astronomical coordinates and measurements of planets in traditional astrology.
91. **Succedent House** — The 2nd, 5th, 8th, and 11th houses of a birth chart, traditionally associated in astrology with resources, consolidation, stabilization, and internal drive.
92. **Succedent Houses** — Houses 2, 5, 8, and 11; sectors of consolidation, resources, stability, and security.
93. **Table of Houses** — A traditional reference book used to calculate house cusps for specific geographic latitudes before computer software.
94. **Tenth House** — The house of career, public reputation, social status, and major life ambitions. It signifies what a person builds in the outer world and how authority is handled.
95. **Third House** — The house of communication, short journeys, siblings, early education, and daily environment. It describes how the mind processes information and interacts with the immediate neighborhood.
96. **Topocentric Houses** — A modern variation of the Placidus house system adjusted for the observer's exact location on Earth.
97. **Tropic of Cancer** — The northernmost circle of latitude on Earth where the Sun can be directly overhead.
98. **Tropic of Capricorn** — The southernmost circle of latitude on Earth where the Sun can be directly overhead.
99. **Twelfth House** — The final cadent house of the chart, traditionally associated with hidden matters, solitude, the subconscious, spirituality, and self-undoing.
100. **Twilight** — The transitional time of day before sunrise or after sunset, historically important for calculating certain charts.
101. **Universal Time** — The international time standard often used as a baseline for casting accurate global birth charts.
102. **Vernal Equinox** — The exact moment the Sun crosses the celestial equator going north, marking the zero-degree point of Aries and the astrological new year.
103. **Whole Sign Houses** — House system where the entire sign containing the Ascendant becomes the 1st house, and each subsequent sign forms a full house.
104. **Zenith** — The point directly overhead an observer on Earth (distinct from the Midheaven, which lies on the ecliptic).

## Zodiac Signs, Elements & Modalities

105. **Air Element** — Triplicity associated with intellect, communication, social exchange, and abstraction (Gemini, Libra, Aquarius).
106. **Air Signs** — The zodiac signs Gemini, Libra, and Aquarius, which are traditionally associated in astrology with intellect, communication, social connection, and abstract thinking.
107. **Anaretic Degree** — The 29° mark (29°00'-29°59') of any sign; associated with urgency, culmination, or crisis.
108. **Aquarius** — The eleventh sign of the zodiac, symbolized by the Water Bearer and traditionally associated in astrology with innovation, community, humanitarian ideals, and nonconformity.
109. **Aries** — The first sign of the zodiac, ruled by Mars, symbolized by the Ram. It is a cardinal fire sign characterized by boldness, initiative, impatience, and pioneering spirit.
110. **Ascending Signs** — Signs of long ascension that take longer than two hours to cross the horizon in the Northern Hemisphere (Cancer to Sagittarius).
111. **Ayanamsa** — The angular difference between the Tropical and Sidereal zodiacs (24° in modern calculations).
112. **Barren Signs** — Signs traditionally linked with low fertility or abstraction (Gemini, Leo, Virgo).
113. **Bicorporeal Signs** — Double-bodied signs representing duality or multiplicity (Gemini, Pisces, Sagittarius, Virgo).
114. **Cancer** — The fourth sign of the zodiac, symbolized by the Crab and traditionally associated in astrology with emotional depth, nurturing, home, and intuition.
115. **Capricorn** — The tenth sign of the zodiac, ruled by Saturn, symbolized by the Sea-Goat. It is a cardinal earth sign known for ambition, pragmatism, responsibility, and disciplined effort.
116. **Cardinal Modality** — Quadruplicity associated with initiation, starting projects, and directional force (Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn).
117. **Cardinal Signs** — The zodiac signs Aries, Cancer, Libra, and Capricorn, which traditionally mark the turning of the seasons and are associated in astrology with initiation, action, and leadership.
118. **Commanding Signs** — Signs from Aries to Virgo, occupying the northern celestial hemisphere.
119. **Common Signs** — An alternative historical term for mutable signs, highlighting their adaptable and transitional nature.
120. **Critical Degrees** — The 0°, 13°, and 26° marks in Cardinal signs; 8° and 21° in Fixed; 4° and 17° in Mutable.
121. **Decan / Decanate** — A 10° subdivision of a sign (3 decans per sign, 36 total across the zodiac).
122. **Descending Signs** — Signs of short ascension that cross the horizon in less than two hours in the Northern Hemisphere (Capricorn to Gemini).
123. **Diurnal Signs** — Masculine/Positive signs characterized by outgoing, expressive energy (Fire and Air signs).
124. **Dwadasamsa (Duad)** — A 2.5° subdivision of a sign (12 duads per sign, 144 total).
125. **Earth Element** — Triplicity associated with materiality, practicality, structure, and sensation (Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn).
126. **Earth Signs** — The zodiac signs Taurus, Virgo, and Capricorn, which are traditionally associated in astrology with the physical world, practicality, manifestation, and sensory experience.
127. **Element** — One of the four fundamental categories (Fire, Earth, Air, Water) that group zodiac signs by shared energetic temperament in astrological theory.
128. **Equinoctial Signs** — Aries and Libra; signs marking the start of equal day and night.
129. **Feminine Signs** — A traditional, non-gendered term for Earth and Water signs, denoting an internal, receptive, and reflective energetic style.
130. **Feral Signs** — Signs represented by wild animals (Leo and the second half of Sagittarius).
131. **Fertile Signs** — Signs traditionally linked with growth and productivity (Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces).
132. **Fire Element** — Triplicity associated with inspiration, vitality, drive, and enthusiasm (Aries, Leo, Sagittarius).
133. **Fire Signs** — The zodiac signs Aries, Leo, and Sagittarius, which are traditionally associated in astrology with inspiration, enthusiasm, energy, and spontaneity.
134. **Fixed Modality** — Quadruplicity associated with stabilization, endurance, resistance to change, and concentration (Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius).
135. **Fixed Signs** — The zodiac signs Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, and Aquarius, which traditionally occur in the middle of seasons and are associated in astrology with stability, endurance, and focus.
136. **Gemini** — The third sign of the zodiac, symbolized by the Twins and traditionally associated in astrology with curiosity, communication, versatility, and mental agility.
137. **Human Signs** — Signs represented by human figures (Gemini, Virgo, Aquarius, and the first half of Sagittarius).
138. **Hydriot** — An archaic or rare term occasionally found in obscure historical translation texts regarding water signs.
139. **Leo** — The fifth sign of the zodiac, symbolized by the Lion and traditionally associated in astrology with creativity, leadership, self-expression, pride, and warmth.
140. **Libra** — The seventh sign of the zodiac, ruled by Venus, symbolized by the Scales. It is a cardinal air sign emphasizing partnership, diplomacy, aesthetics, and the search for balance.
141. **Masculine Signs** — A traditional, non-gendered term for Fire and Air signs, denoting an external, active, and expressive energetic style.
142. **Mutable Modality** — Quadruplicity associated with adaptation, transition, flexibility, and synthesis (Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Pisces).
143. **Mutable Signs** — The zodiac signs Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, and Pisces, which traditionally occur at the end of seasons and are associated in astrology with adaptability, transition, and versatility.
144. **Mute / Inarticulate Signs** — Water signs (Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces), traditionally associated with introversion or non-verbal processing.
145. **Nocturnal Signs** — Feminine/Negative signs characterized by receptive, reflective energy (Earth and Water signs).
146. **Obeying Signs** — Signs from Libra to Pisces, occupying the southern celestial hemisphere.
147. **Pisces** — The twelfth sign of the zodiac, traditionally ruled by Jupiter and modernly by Neptune, symbolized by the Fish. It is a mutable water sign defined by empathy, imagination, spiritual sensitivity, and boundless compassion.
148. **Precession of the Equinoxes** — The slow westward drift of equinoxes along the ecliptic (1° every 72 years) due to Earth's axial wobble.
149. **Quadruped Signs** — A historical term for certain animal zodiac signs like Taurus, Leo, and Aries.

154. **Solstitial Signs** — Cancer and Capricorn; signs marking the maximum northern and southern solar declination.

156. **Tropical Zodiac** — Zodiac based on the Earth-Sun seasonal cycle, fixed to the vernal equinox (0° Aries).
157. **Via Combusta** — The "Burned Way"; historically designated between 15° Libra and 15° Scorpio.
158. **Virgo** — The sixth sign of the zodiac, symbolized by the Maiden and traditionally associated in astrology with service, organization, analysis, health, and craftsmanship.
159. **Water Element** — Triplicity associated with emotion, intuition, memory, and subjective experience (Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces).
160. **Water Signs** — The zodiac signs Cancer, Scorpio, and Pisces, which are traditionally associated in astrology with emotion, intuition, empathy, and the psychic realm.
161. **Zodiac** — The imaginary 360-degree band of the heavens divided into twelve equal signs through which the planets appear to travel.

## Essential Dignities, Debilities & Classical Conditions

162. **Accidental Debility** — A planet's weakness caused by poor house placement, retrograde motion, or hard aspects.
163. **Accidental Dignity** — A planet's situational strength derived from house placement, aspectual backing, speed, or lack of combustion.
164. **Almuten** — A specific planet in a birth chart that has the strongest overall essential and accidental dignity, making it a primary ruler of the chart according to traditional astrology.
165. **Almuten Figuris** — The single planet holding the highest combined essential and accidental dignity score in a natal chart.
166. **Angular Motion** — The speed at which a planet appears to move along the zodiac during a given timeframe.
167. **Apparent Motion** — The illusion of planetary movement viewed from the vantage point of Earth.
168. **Avayogi** — A challenging counter-point to the Yogi point in Vedic astrology.
169. **Benefit** — A traditional term often applied to Jupiter and Venus, viewed in classical astrology as planets that bring ease, growth, and favorable outcomes.
170. **Besieged** — A traditional condition where a planet is caught between two malefic planets, such as Mars and Saturn, by aspect or placement. It suggests feeling trapped or pressured from multiple difficult directions.
171. **Bonification** — A traditional condition where a planet is strengthened by favorable aspects from benefics like Venus or Jupiter.
172. **Bound** — Also known as a term, this is a traditional division of a zodiac sign into unequal segments ruled by one of the five visible planets, adding fine-tuned dignity to a placement.
173. **Bound Ruler** — The planetary steward of a specific subdivision within a zodiac sign, influencing the planet residing in those degrees.
174. **Cazimi** — A planet within 17 arcminutes (0°17') of the Sun's center; considered greatly empowered ("in the heart of the Sun").
175. **Chaldean Order** — Ancient arrangement of planets by orbital speed: Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon.
176. **Combustion** — A planet within 8.5° of the Sun; obscured and functionally weakened by solar intensity.
177. **Daily Motion** — The average distance a planet travels through the zodiac in a single 24-hour period.
178. **Day Chart** — A chart born while the Sun is above the horizon, which changes how diurnal and nocturnal planets are evaluated in traditional astrology.
179. **Day Ruler** — The planet that rules the planetary hours starting from sunrise on a given day of the week, connecting days to their classical planetary names like Saturn's Day or Sun's Day.
180. **Detriment** — The sign opposite a planet's domicile, where its mechanical expression encounters friction.
181. **Direct Motion** — Normal forward movement of a planet through the zodiac from west to east.
182. **Dispositor** — The planet that rules the zodiac sign where another planet is located, creating a chain of command used in astrological analysis to find ultimate motivators.
183. **Diurnal** — Relating to the daytime or to planets that function most comfortably during the day in traditional Hellenistic astrology.
184. **Domicile** — The sign ruled by a planet, where it operates with full agency and natural expression.
185. **Doriphoros** — A planet acting as a spear-bearer or bodyguard, rising immediately before the Sun.
186. **Doryphory** — Grouping of planets attending the Sun or Moon to enhance natal status.
187. **Essential Debility** — A planet's weakness derived from being in detriment or fall.
188. **Essential Dignity** — The inherent strength or affinity of a planet based on its zodiacal sign placement.
189. **Exaltation** — A sign placement where a planet expresses its highest, most refined or elevated potential.
190. **Faces** — The 36 decans assigned traditional planetary rulers according to the Chaldean order.
191. **Fall** — The sign opposite a planet's exaltation, where its primary drives operate at a functional disadvantage.
192. **Halb** — A planet positioned above or below the horizon in accordance with its diurnal or nocturnal sect alignment.
193. **Hayz** — A traditional dignity where a diurnal planet is above the horizon by day in a masculine sign (or nocturnal below by night in feminine).
194. **Joy of the Planets** — A traditional concept where each planet finds a special affinity or happiness in a specific house.
195. **Malefic** — A traditional term applied to Mars and Saturn, viewed in classical astrology as planets that bring friction, restriction, difficulty, and hard lessons.
196. **Maltreatment** — A traditional condition where a planet suffers stress through harsh aspects from malefics like Mars or Saturn.
197. **Manglik** — A Vedic condition where Mars is placed in specific houses, traditionally associated with marriage compatibility considerations.
198. **Modern Ruler** — The newly assigned planetary ruler for certain zodiac signs discovered after the invention of the telescope (Uranus for Aquarius, Neptune for Pisces, Pluto for Scorpio).
199. **Mutual Reception** — A condition where two planets reside in each other's dignities (e.g., Sun in Scorpio, Mars in Leo).
200. **Mutual Reception by Exaltation** — A supportive relationship where two planets occupy each other's exaltation signs.
201. **Natural Ruler** — The modern association linking each zodiac sign to its corresponding planetary ruler and house.
202. **Nocturnal** — Relating to the night-time or to planets that function most comfortably during the night in traditional Hellenistic astrology.
203. **Occidental** — A planet setting after the Sun in the evening sky.
204. **Orb** — The allowable margin of error or degree variance permitted for an astrological aspect to still be considered active and influential.
205. **Oriental** — A planet rising before the Sun in the morning sky.
206. **Peregrine** — A planet occupying a position where it holds no essential dignity (Domicile, Exaltation, Triplicity, Term, or Face).
207. **Planetary Day** — A traditional system where each day of the week is named after and ruled by a specific traditional planet.
208. **Planetary Hour** — An ancient time-dividing system where each hour of the day and night is ruled by a specific planet in a continuous repeating sequence. It is often used in electional astrology to time activities.
209. **Reception** — A traditional astrological condition where one planet occupies a sign ruled by another, creating a supportive exchange of energies between them.
210. **Retrograde Motion (Retrograde)** — Apparent backward movement of a planet across the background stars caused by orbital relative velocities.
211. **Ruler** — The planet that has traditional governance over a specific zodiac sign or astrological house.
212. **Sect** — The division of charts and planets into Diurnal (Day: Sun, Jupiter, Saturn) and Nocturnal (Night: Moon, Venus, Mars) teams.
213. **Shadow Period** — The span of zodiacal degrees across which a planet travels during its retrograde cycle.
214. **Significator** — A designated planet or house ruler in a chart that represents a specific person, object, or question in traditional astrology.
215. **Slow in Course** — A planet traveling slower than its average daily mean motion.
216. **Slow Planet** — Outer planets like Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto that spend many years in a single sign, shaping generational trends.
217. **Station** — The brief period when a planet appears motionless in the sky before changing direction from direct to retrograde or vice versa, amplifying its symbolic impact.
218. **Stationary Direct** — The precise moment a planet appears to halt before resuming direct forward motion.
219. **Stationary Retrograde (Retrograde Station)** — The precise moment a planet appears to halt before beginning retrograde motion.
220. **Swift in Course** — A planet traveling faster than its average daily mean motion.
221. **Terms / Bounds** — Division of each zodiac sign into five unequal sections ruled by the five traditional planets.
222. **Triplicity** — A traditional dignity system based on the four elements, where different planets rule a sign by day or by night.
223. **Triplicity Ruler** — Planet governing a sign element based on whether the chart is set for day or night.
224. **Under the Sunbeams** — A planet within 8.5° to 17° of the Sun; experiencing minor solar debilitation.

## Aspects, Geometry & Chart Configurations

225. **Abscission of Light** — Interruption of an applying aspect before completion due to a faster planet intervening or changing direction.
226. **Application by Latitude** — An aspect formed using celestial latitude rather than zodiacal longitude, common in advanced traditional work.
227. **Applying Aspect** — Aspect where a faster planet moves toward exact alignment with a slower planet.

482. **Intuitive Astrology** — A contemporary approach that blends traditional chart mechanics with psychic or intuitive interpretation methods.
483. **Jyotish (Vedic Astrology)** — The traditional Indian sidereal astrological system utilizing Nakshatras and Dasha periods.
484. **Karmic Astrology** — A specialized branch of astrology focused on past lives, soul purpose, spiritual debts, and the evolutionary path of the individual.
485. **Local Space Astrology** — Relocation framework mapping planetary azimuth directions directly across a town or home layout.
486. **Locational Astrology** — Broad umbrella term covering Astrocartography, Relocation, and Local Space methods.
487. **Medical Astrology** — Branch mapping physical anatomy, vitality, and health tendencies through planetary configurations.
488. **Medieval Astrology** — Arabic and European practices (8th to 14th century) emphasizing dignities and horary work.
489. **Meteorological Astrology (Astrometeorology)** — System predicting weather patterns through planetary ingress and aspect cycles.
490. **Modern Astrology** — The psychological and evolutionary approach to astrology that developed heavily in the 20th century.
491. **Monad** — An esoteric term sometimes used to describe the indivisible unity of a chart's core essence.
492. **Mundane Astrology** — The branch analyzing political events, nations, socio-economic trends, and natural events.
493. **Nadi** — A complex system of pulse diagnosis or palm-leaf astrology, also referring to energy channels in Vedic thought.
494. **Nakshatra** — One of the twenty-seven lunar mansions used in Vedic astrology to divide the zodiac into smaller, nuanced psychological and predictive sectors. Each nakshatra is governed by a deity and a planetary lord.
495. **Natal Astrology** — The study of an individual's birth chart (_radix_) to analyze psychology, potential, and life patterns.
496. **Navamsha** — The ninth harmonic chart in Vedic astrology, dividing each sign into nine parts to reveal deep marriage compatibility, spiritual destiny, and inner strengths. It is considered one of the most important divisional charts.
497. **Padas** — The four quarters into which each of the 27 Nakshatras is divided.
498. **Pentacle** — A geometric five-pointed star shape occasionally linked to esoteric astrology and Venusian cycles.
499. **Psychological Astrology** — Synthesis of astrological archetypes with depth psychology (Jungian concepts).
500. **Rashi** — The term for a zodiac sign in Vedic astrology.
501. **Sidereal Astrology** — An astrological system that uses the actual fixed constellations of stars rather than the seasonal tropical zodiac for its calculations.
502. **Temperament** — The ancient classification of a person's physical and psychological makeup based on the balance of the four elements and humors in the chart.
503. **Traditional Astrology** — An umbrella term for pre-modern astrological systems, including Hellenistic, Medieval, and Renaissance practices.
504. **Uranian Astrology** — System developed by Alfred Witte utilizing 90-degree dials, midpoints, and hypothetical planets.
505. **Valens** — Referring to Vettius Valens, an ancient Hellenistic astrologer whose surviving writings are foundational to traditional astrology.
506. **Varga Chart** — A divisional chart in Vedic astrology used to zoom in on specific areas of life like marriage or career.
507. **Wind Rose** — A directional chart occasionally used in mundane or locational astrology.
508. **Yellow Ray** — An esoteric ray categorization linked to certain philosophical zodiac signs in esoteric astrology.
509. **Yogi** — A sensitive point in Vedic astrology calculated by adding the Sun and Moon's longitudes.

## Interpretative Patterns, Lots & Advanced Terminology

510. **Alcochoden** — Traditional planet designated as the giver of years, used alongside the Hyleg to estimate longevity.
511. **Altitude** — The vertical height of a celestial body measured in degrees above or below the horizon.
512. **Anareta** — The "killing planet"; traditionally identified as a planet causing physical challenge or life crisis.
513. **Annual Motion** — Earth's yearly revolution around the Sun, causing the Sun's apparent movement through the zodiac.
514. **Anomalistic Cycle** — Orbital period measured from perihelion to perihelion (closest point to Sun).
515. **Arabic Parts / Lots** — Calculated points derived by adding and subtracting chart longitudes (Point A + Point B − Point C).
516. **Avatar Degrees** — The fixed mid-points of Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, and Aquarius, associated with spiritual awakening and turning points.
517. **Azimuth** — The compass bearing (0° to 360°) of a sky object measured along the horizon from North.
518. **Birth Chart** — A circular map of the heavens calculated for the exact date, time, and location of a person's birth, serving as the core tool of astrological interpretation.
519. **Boomerang** — An aspect pattern consisting of a T-square supported by an additional planet forming a sesquiquadrate and semi-square to resolve the tension. It channels internal crisis into focused, productive outlets.
520. **Bowl Pattern** — Chart shape where all planets are concentrated within one half (180°) of the wheel.
521. **Bucket Pattern** — Chart shape where nine planets lie within a 180° arc, opposed by a single isolated "handle" planet.
522. **Bundle Pattern** — Chart shape where all planets are concentrated within a 120° arc.
523. **Chart Ruler** — Standard term for the planet governing the sign of the Ascendant.
524. **Cradle** — An aspect pattern resembling a basket or boat shape, featuring a cluster of sextiles capped by an opposition. It offers a balanced structure for navigating complex emotional or psychological currents.
525. **Degreeology** — The practice of reading the specific numerical degree of a planet to glean extra meaning through numerology or traditional decans.
526. **Derivation (Turned Chart)** — The method of using houses in relation to other houses, such as viewing the seventh house as the tenth from the tenth (career of a partner).
527. **Direct Midpoint** — Condition where a third planet physically occupies the exact midpoint degree of two other planets.
528. **Dispositor Chain** — Map showing how planets rule one another based on their sign placements.
529. **Diurnal Motion** — The daily apparent 360° rotation of the sky caused by Earth's spin on its axis.
530. **Draconic Cycle** — Orbital period measured between consecutive crossings of the North Node.
531. **Elemental Balance** — The distribution of chart placements across the four elements: fire, earth, air, and water.
532. **Final Dispositor** — Single planet ruling all other planets through a chain of dispositorship (e.g., Mars in Aries ruling everything).
533. **Geminate** — A term occasionally used to describe twin charts or paired planetary placements.
534. **Geocentric** — Perspective centered on Earth; standard perspective for natal and predictive astrological charts.
535. **Gestalt / Chart Shape** — Global pattern formed by planetary distribution across the chart wheel (Marc Edmund Jones classification).
536. **Giver of Life** — Synonym for the Hyleg point in traditional charts.
537. **Great Mutation** — The shift of Jupiter-Saturn conjunctions into a new elemental triplicity.
538. **Heliocentric** — Perspective centered on the Sun; used in specialized macro-cycle and transit studies.
539. **Hemisphere Emphasis** — The concentration of planets in specific quadrants of the chart, revealing whether a person focuses more on the inner self, public life, independence, or relationships.
540. **Horoscope** — A chart or diagram of the heavens cast for a specific time and place to interpret astrological themes, derived from the Greek words for "hour-marker."
541. **Hyleg** — Traditional point designated as the giver of life and physical vitality in a natal chart.
542. **Indirect Midpoint** — Condition where a third planet forms a hard aspect (45°, 90°, 135°, 180°) to a midpoint.
543. **Karma** — A philosophical concept often integrated into modern and karmic astrology, suggesting that current chart placements reflect lessons and patterns carried over from past actions or soul evolution.
544. **Locomotive Pattern** — Chart shape where planets span across 240°, leaving an open 120° gap.
545. **Lord of the Geniture** — Planet holding overall dominant influence or authority over a chart.
546. **Lot of Courage** — A traditional Arabic part linked to daring, boldness, and martial drive.
547. **Low-Water** — A colloquial slang term sometimes used in traditional readings to describe a debilitated placement.
548. **Midpoint** — The exact mathematical halfway point between two celestial bodies ((λ₁ + λ₂) / 2).
549. **Midpoint Tree** — Structured list of all planetary pairs sharing a common midpoint axis.
550. **Natal Chart** — The formal astrological term for a birth chart.
551. **Nativity** — An archaic term for a birth chart or the actual birth of an individual.
552. **Ophiuchus** — Constellation intersecting the ecliptic between Scorpio and Sagittarius (not a zodiac sign in tropical astrology).
553. **Out-of-Bounds (OOB)** — Condition where a planet's declination exceeds the Sun's maximum declination (23°26' North or South).
554. **Parans (Paranatellonta)** — Star-planet co-risings or co-culminations taking place at the same latitude simultaneously.
555. **Polar Significance** — The symbolic focus given to high-latitude charts where standard house divisions behave unusually.
556. **Polarity** — The natural opposition between two signs located directly across from one another on the zodiac wheel, such as Taurus and Scorpio.
557. **Quadrant Emphasis** — The concentration of planets in one of the four chart quadrants, indicating whether life energy is directed toward personal discovery, relational growth, societal contribution, or spiritual transcendence.
558. **Quality** — The three-fold categorization of zodiac signs into Cardinal, Fixed, and Mutable based on how they handle momentum and change.
559. **Radix** — The original birth chart; the root reference point for all subsequent predictive work.
560. **Ruler of the Ascendant** — Planet governing the sign on the 1st house cusp; acts as the primary driver of the native.
561. **Sabian Symbols** — A set of 360 evocative visual scenes corresponding to each individual degree of the zodiac wheel.
562. **Seesaw Pattern** — Chart shape where planets cluster into two opposing groups separated by empty space.
563. **Sidereal Period** — Time taken for a body to complete one full orbit around the Sun relative to fixed stars.
564. **Sinistra** — A historical term referring to movement against the natural order of the zodiac.
565. **Splash Pattern** — Chart shape where planets are evenly distributed across all twelve signs.
566. **Splay Pattern** — Chart shape characterized by irregular clusters of planets separated by wide gaps.
567. **Synodic Period** — Time taken for a planet to return to the same position relative to the Sun as seen from Earth.
568. **Tempera** — A classical concept regarding the blending of elemental humors in traditional medical astrology.
569. **Theme** — The overarching narrative or psychological pattern suggested by a birth chart's prominent features.
570. **Topocentric** — Perspective calculated from the exact geographical surface position of the observer rather than Earth's center.
571. **Vocation** — The life calling or career path indicated by the 10th house, Midheaven, and related chart factors.
572. **Void of Course (VoC)** — State of the Moon after making its last major Ptolemaic aspect in its current sign before leaving it.
573. **Wholeness** — The psychological ideal of integrating all conflicting parts of a birth chart into a balanced personality.
