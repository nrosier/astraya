What a key is

Every corpus entry is identified by a key, derived from the placement it describes by placementKey and never typed by hand. The key’s first segment is the category; the rest says which placement. There is one entry per key and language.

The numbers are plain integers, and the two kinds are easy to confuse: a sign is a zero-based index (0 = Aries … 11 = Pisces), a house is one-based (1–12). So planet-in-sign:sun:2 is the Sun in Gemini, while planet-in-house:sun:2 is the Sun in the 2nd house. Bodies are BodyDefinition.keys (sun, meanNode…) and aspects are ASPECTS keys (square…).

A person never needs to read a key: the admin screens show what an entry means in words, with the key as small secondary text (labelForKey in src/ui/placement-label.ts, #428).

Ordering

  • aspect-pair and synastry-aspect are stored once per unordered pair, with the two bodies in alphabetical order. A symmetric aspect needs one entry, not two, and a key in the other order is refused as non-canonical rather than becoming a second entry.
  • A synastry-aspect text is written from the first body’s owner: “your Mars … their Moon” for …:mars:moon. A screen that holds the pair the other way round looks up the same entry and says whose side it speaks from (src/ui/synastry-text.ts, #422). Storing both directions would double 2,046 entries to say the same thing from the other side, so the schema does not.
  • transit-aspect is ordered by role: the transiting body first, the natal body second. The two may even be the same body (a Saturn return).
  • sign-on-cusp puts the sign first and the house second, while planet-in-house puts the body first. Neither is wrong; this table is the reference.

Angle vocabulary

Three spellings of the chart angles exist, each in its own place, with one mapping between the first two (ACG_ANGLE_POINT_KEYS in schema.ts):

Where Spelling Why
Corpus astro-line keys AC DC MC IC Kept: renaming would change every override and the generator’s batches for no reader-visible gain.
Point keys: the AI payload (FocusAngle), the chart tables asc dsc mc ic The same lowercase style every other body and point key uses.
Screens names from astro-names: Ascendant, Midheaven… What a person reads, in their language.

Reserved categories

nakshatra and pattern are accepted by the schema, but no entry, screen or generator uses them yet. profected-house and astro-line are used: the Profections screen shows the text of the profected house, and the Astrocartography screen shows what each checked body means on each checked line.

Composite categories

composite-planet-in-sign, composite-planet-in-house and composite-aspect-pair are the composite chart's own siblings of planet-in-sign, planet-in-house and aspect-pair — same shape, same body/sign/house/aspect coverage, read by src/interpretation/report.ts's assembleReport whenever chartKind is 'composite'. A composite's placements describe the relationship or combination itself, not either person individually, so they need their own generated text rather than reusing the natal category's "you"/"your" voice — the same reason synastry-aspect is its own category rather than a qualified aspect-pair. sign-on-cusp and dignity-state have no composite sibling: a house cusp's sign and a planet's essential dignity are facts about the chart's own structure, not a trait attributed to "you", so the plain natal category already reads fine for a composite chart too.

How this is kept true

  • test/interpretation-key-reference.test.ts holds one example per category, its shape and its ordering rule, and fails if any of them changes.
  • npm run corpus:audit (tools/corpus-gen/lib/corpus-audit.mjs) checks the committed corpus: every key valid and canonical, no duplicates, English and Dutch holding the same keys, nothing outside the generator’s scope, and the placements the generator expects but the corpus lacks. A theme check compares each text’s subject with its key’s own sign or house, so a shifted index (the Sun in Gemini filed as Taurus) would fail even though every key is individually valid. The same audit runs in the test suite (test/tools-corpus-audit.test.ts) and is meant to be run after every regeneration.
